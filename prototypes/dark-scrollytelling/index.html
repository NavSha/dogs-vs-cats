Chapter 2 · prototype

The machine
looks for patterns.

A grid of numbers isn’t much to go on. So it passes your photo through a stack of layers — and each one hunts for something a little more complex than the last. Scroll to watch them light up.

scroll
00 / your photo

This is all it gets.

One small photo, frozen at 150×150 pixels. No labels, no context — just colour. Everything the machine is about to “understand” has to be squeezed out of this.

01 / the first layer

It starts with edges.

The very first layer is almost dumb. It only reacts to sharp boundaries — places where light meets dark. Watch the glow trace the outline of the animal and ignore the flat areas.

That’s a layer: one step of pattern-finding, whose output feeds the next.

02 / deeper in

Edges become textures.

A few layers later, those edges have been combined into texture — fur, grass, the grain of things. The map gets coarser, because each point now summarises a whole patch of the original photo.

03 / deeper still

Textures become parts.

Deeper again, the machine is lighting up on blobs and parts — the rough place where a snout sits, or an ear. It has never been told what an ear is. It just learned that this shape predicts “dog.”

Stack a few dozen of these, and you get a dog.

No single layer understands anything. The understanding is in the stacking — simple patterns becoming complex ones, until the last layer can simply say which it is.
